The invitation arrived on a Tuesday, folded inside a square of silver cloth that no one in Threadhaven could identify.

Mara turned it over in her hands — the fabric first, then the letter inside. The handwriting was careful, each letter formed as though the writer had thought twice before committing it to the page. She had seen that handwriting before. Once, on a note left tucked beneath her loom. Once, on a name stitched into the lining of a coat that had appeared on her doorstep in the middle of winter.

She knew who it was from before she read a word of it.

*You are invited,* it said, *to a wedding.*

The rest of the details were ordinary enough — a date, a village three days' walk north, a name she didn't recognise beside the name she did. She read it twice. Then she sat down on the low bench by the hearth and read it a third time, because sometimes words rearranged themselves into something more sensible if you gave them the chance.

They didn't.

She folded the letter back along its creases and held it in her lap. Outside, the looms of Threadhaven hummed their morning song. The silver thread — her thread, the one she kept wound on a small bone spool and never used for anything, because she didn't know what it was for — caught the light from the window and gleamed once, briefly, like a signal.

*A wedding,* she thought.

She was going to need better shoes.
