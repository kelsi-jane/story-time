# I'll Lead

The thing about Luna is that she never just tells you the answer. I've figured this out now, but sitting on that log in the gray, leaf-scattered woods with a chipmunk beside me and a fox probably already thinking about acorns, it was a little frustrating. I wanted her to say: *here's the path, here's the way, follow me.* Instead she gave me the question and waited.

*What do you think?*

I thought about it. Seriously, actually thought about it. My heart rate was coming back down from the Pip Incident, and I was starting to look at the situation instead of just feeling it. We weren't that deep in — we'd been walking less than an hour. The sun, now slipping back out from behind the clouds, was over my left shoulder, southwest-ish and lower than it was when we'd started. Which meant west was roughly that direction. Which meant the house was east.

I also noticed the ground had a slope to it. Very slight, but there — downhill from where I was sitting. Knox had said the Heart Tree was dead center of the woods. Water runs downhill and collects in low places. If there was a creek in the middle, downhill would take us toward it. And toward the center.

"I think," I said slowly, "that Knox has been walking us in a loop because he's been foraging and not actually navigating."

"An extremely hurtful and accurate statement," Knox said from behind a tree.

"And I think if we go downhill, we'll hit the creek, and the creek will lead us to the center."

Silence. Then Autumn said, "...Yeah, that tracks, actually."

Luna made a small sound that wasn't quite a word but felt like approval. Pip finished whatever he'd been eating — produced from absolutely nowhere — and looked at me with his enormous eyes. "So you're leading now?"

"I'm leading now."

Knox appeared from behind the tree. He looked at me with those amber eyes — steady, not teasing for once — and said: "You know, you've been navigating this whole time. You just didn't know it."

I didn't totally understand what he meant right then. (I do now. That's the thing about Knox — his dad jokes are obvious, but his actual wisdom is sneaky.) I stood up, brushed bark off my jeans, and picked a direction.

The ground sloped slightly left and forward. I went left and forward.

Nobody argued. Autumn fell into step beside me, ears forward, whole body vibrating with enthusiasm now that we had a destination and a direction. Pip decided, without being asked, that he was coming too, and installed himself on my shoulder — warm and weighing almost nothing, occasionally pointing at things that interested him, which was: most things.

Knox walked slightly behind us. I'd glance back sometimes and catch him watching with this expression I couldn't quite read. Not proud, exactly. More like *interested* — like he was watching something happen that he'd been looking forward to.

The woods felt different now that I was the one choosing the path. Not less wild, not less shadowy — but *mine,* somehow. Like they'd shifted from a place I was moving through to a place I was moving *in.* The creak of branches sounded less like warnings and more like company. The light slanted through the canopy and landed in exactly the places I was about to step.

I was still a little scared. But I was starting to think that scared-and-moving-forward is just called *going,* and going was more than enough.

We kept going.

The sound of the creek reached us before we could see it — a low, steady murmur threading through the trees — and Autumn let out this joyful little bark and took off at a run, and I laughed and ran after her, leaves exploding up around our feet, and for that minute the whole forest felt like it was running with us.
