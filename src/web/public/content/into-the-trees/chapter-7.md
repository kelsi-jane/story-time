# The Heart Tree

We found it just before the light went orange.

And when I say we found it, I mostly mean it found us — because suddenly the forest opened up into a wide, still clearing, and there it was: an oak so old and so enormous that it seemed less like a tree and more like a small country. The trunk was wider than my bedroom, easy, with deeply furrowed bark gone gray-brown and mossy in the crevices. Roots spilled out from its base in great wave-like ridges, making natural seats and shelves and little curved hollows you could settle right into. The canopy spread out overhead like a ceiling that had decided to be the whole sky instead. A few leaves were still holding on up there — stubborn, golden, catching the last of the afternoon — and the rest had come down in a wide, soft ring around the base, like the tree had arranged them.

I walked up to it slowly. I put both hands on the bark.

Rough and cool and absolutely, solidly real.

I waited. In my head, I'd been expecting something. A whisper. A warm glow. Some mystical feeling like the forest was *choosing me,* recognizing me, saying: *yes, this is the one.* I'd read enough stories to have exactly that expectation, and I am not embarrassed to admit it.

Nothing whispered. The tree just was.

But here's the thing: standing there with my palms against that bark, in that clearing, with the gold light coming down and Autumn chasing a blowing leaf somewhere behind me and Knox doing Knox things in the roots and Luna settled somewhere above — I felt something. It wasn't what I expected. It was quieter than that, and more mine.

A sense of *here.* Just: here. Me, in these woods, in this body, on this day. Not performing being Kelsi, not auditioning for some future version of herself, not scrolling through all the possible things she might become. Just: here, boots muddy, hair a mess, laughing at a log bridge like it was the best thing that had ever happened to her.

That was actually sort of everything, I realized.

"You didn't hear it," said a very slow voice from somewhere in the roots.

I looked down. Tucked into a curve of root, almost exactly the color of fallen leaves, was a tortoise. Deeply, genuinely old — the kind of old that has weight to it — with a domed shell and eyes like polished stones.

"I didn't hear anything," I said.

"Mm," said the tortoise, and paused for a long moment that felt less like silence and more like punctuation. "Most don't." Another pause. "Not the first time."

"So I have to come back?"

He blinked. Very, very slowly. "You'll come back," he said, "because you want to. That's different."

I thought about this. "That's kind of a better reason."

"Yes," he said simply, and closed his eyes. Apparently finished.

Autumn flopped into the roots beside me, looked up at the canopy like it was the most wonderful ceiling she'd ever seen, and said: "Worth it?"

"Yeah," I said. "Really worth it."

I sat in the roots of the Heart Tree while the light shifted from gold to amber to the soft purple-gray of late afternoon, and I turned the day over in my mind: the circles we walked, the clouds rolling in, the moment I'd just picked a direction and gone. The log and the cold water and the laugh I couldn't stop. All the times I'd almost turned back. All the times I hadn't.

I didn't know who I was yet. That was still true. But sitting there, I was starting to think that was okay — that finding yourself wasn't a moment you arrived at but a direction you moved in, and as long as you kept moving, you were already doing it. You were already becoming.

I traced the bark with my fingers.

"Same time next week?" I asked Knox.

"I'll have a new route," he said, without looking up from whatever he was investigating in the roots.

"I'll bring snacks," Autumn said.

Above us, somewhere in the gold and the leaves, Luna made a sound like a soft, contented exhale.

I stayed until the first star appeared between the branches, a small cold point of light in the deepening blue. Then I stood up, brushed off my jeans, and walked home through the dusk — boots still damp, cheeks cold, heart entirely full.

The woods watched me go.

I was pretty sure they'd be there when I came back.
