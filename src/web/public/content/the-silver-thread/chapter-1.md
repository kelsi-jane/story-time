The looms always sang before dawn.

Mara had grown up with that sound — a soft, rhythmic hum that crept through the floorboards and curled under her blanket like a cat seeking warmth. By the time she was old enough to understand it, she had stopped noticing it entirely. The loom was simply there, the way breathing was simply there, the way the grey-violet sky above Threadhaven was simply there every morning when she opened her shutters.

This morning, it was different.

She felt it before she heard it — a change in the vibration, subtle as a key shifting in a song. She sat up in bed, the cold air sharp against her face, and listened. The loom was still singing, yes. But underneath its familiar rhythm, there was something else. A second voice. Thinner, higher, almost too faint to name.

*Silver,* she thought, though she didn't know why.

She dressed quickly, by feel in the dark, and padded downstairs.

The weaving room was dim, lit only by the embers in the hearth and the pale band of pre-dawn light pressing through the east window. Her mother's loom stood at the centre of the room, vast and dark as a ship's hull. Mara's own, smaller loom leaned against the south wall where she had left it the night before, half-threaded with the deep blue wool her mother had measured out for her.

Except.

She crossed the room slowly, as though quick movement might frighten it away. There, running through the blue wool — woven between the warp threads as if it had always been there — was a single thread of silver. Bright as a new coin. Bright as ice in sunlight. Not her mother's. Not from any spool she recognised.

Mara reached out and touched it.

The loom stopped humming.

And from somewhere very far away — or perhaps from somewhere very close, inside the walls, inside the air itself — she heard, just once, a sound that might have been a voice.

It said her name.
